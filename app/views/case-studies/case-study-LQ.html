L&Q: Using training, specialist care and ‘champions’ to improve menopause support

At a glance
Employer name L&Q
Employer sector Housing
Number of employees 1,000 - 4,999
Action category Menopause
Summary of action and implementation L&Q, a large UK social housing provider, successfully improved workplace menopause support by building an open culture and providing practical resources. To achieve this, they introduced Menopause Cafés for safe discussions, trained managers to handle sensitive conversations, offered specialist GP consultations, and established a network of volunteer Menopause Champions. These efforts reduced stigma, boosted employee confidence, and earned L&Q “Menopause Friendly” accreditation as well as an award at the 2026 British Workplace Wellbeing Awards. The case study shows that visible leadership and flexible support are essential for lasting cultural change.

Background Information

L&Q is one of the UK’s largest charitable housing associations and social housing providers. Our purpose is to provide social homes and landlord services that everyone can be proud of. We house around 250,000 people in more than 105,000 homes. Our work spans:

  • housing management
  • construction
  • customer services
  • community investment
  • neighbourhood support
  • specialist care services

We employ a diverse workforce whose roles range from office-based professional functions to frontline operational, customer-facing and maintenance roles.

What they wanted to do

Our menopause programme was developed to enhance support for colleagues in a way that is realistic, evidence-based and aligned with our values. The objectives below reflect what we set out to achieve, informed by conversations with:

  • colleagues in HR, wellbeing, and health and safety
  • our Menopause Champions
  • our equality, diversity and inclusion (EDI) team

These objectives are intentionally focused on principles rather than being driven by data. They recognise the practical limitations around menopause-specific tracking.

Our first objective was to ensure colleagues experiencing menopause symptoms feel supported, informed and able to remain comfortable and effective at work. Menopause can have emotional, cognitive and physical impacts, and our aim was to remove stigma and offer accessible support.

Our next objective was to build an inclusive culture where menopause can be discussed openly. We were aiming to normalise menopause across the organisation and create psychologically safe spaces for conversation.

Our final objective was to improve awareness and accessibility of menopause support and adjustment pathways. We wanted to increase awareness of the full range of support available.

The ultimate ‘so what’ behind this is at the heart of good practice. This was about showing up for colleagues and supporting them in meaningful ways. Many women reach menopause at a point where they have built strong experience that is vital to an organisation’s success. Without the right support in place, companies risk losing their valuable skills and knowledge.

How they did it

Early work began when gaps were identified in the availability and visibility of menopause information and support. These concerns were raised through existing wellbeing and health and safety governance structures, where it was recognised that colleagues were experiencing symptoms without clear guidance, consistent support or confidence in how to raise the topic at work.

Initial activity focused on awareness-raising and education. Communications campaigns were introduced to share information, challenge stigma and signpost colleagues to credible external resources. This early visibility was important in establishing menopause as a legitimate workplace wellbeing topic rather than a private or hidden issue. At this stage, activity was deliberately light-touch and exploratory.

As awareness grew, menopause was formally incorporated into L&Q’s wider wellbeing framework. This step ensured menopause was recognised as part of physical and emotional health rather than treated as a standalone initiative.

Over time, the approach evolved into a more structured and visible programme. A network of menopause champions was established, with colleagues volunteering from a range of departments and role types. Champions play a key role in raising awareness, providing peer support and signposting colleagues to appropriate resources. To complement this, we introduced regular menopause cafés. These gave colleagues access to informal, judgement-free spaces where they can share experiences, ask questions and learn from each other. These cafés became a consistent and trusted touchpoint for engagement, helping to normalise conversation and reduce stigma.

Menopause awareness has also been embedded into wider organisational activity rather than being confined to a single campaign or moment. This includes participation in wellbeing initiatives, learning events and national awareness days, helping to maintain visibility throughout the year. Leadership support has played an important role.

Clinical support forms an important part of the overall offer. Colleagues can access confidential specialist menopause GP consultations, providing medical advice, treatment guidance where appropriate and behavioural support. This clinical pathway complements workplace support and ensures colleagues have access to credible, evidence-based care alongside peer and managerial support.

The outcome

Becoming a menopause friendly organisation has delivered cultural, engagement and wellbeing benefits across the business. While our programme continues to evolve, the outcomes demonstrate meaningful progress in awareness, confidence and support for colleagues experiencing menopause.

Menopause is now a normalised topic across L&Q. The CEO’s promotion of the Menopause Café during an all-staff meeting marked a significant shift, embedding menopause into organisation-wide dialogue. Champions, networks and wellbeing communications have made menopause more visible and relatable, reducing stigma and encouraging colleagues to talk openly. Colleague feedback shows growing confidence in discussing menopause both peer-to-peer and with managers.

There has been strong engagement from colleagues with menopause support initiatives. Data shows that many people are accessing resources, with 52 colleagues attending Menopause Cafes between April and July 2025. One colleague shared how the cafés have helped them support others more effectively:

“The information and support I’ve received through the Menopause Cafés has been really impactful both personally and professionally. I now share what I’ve learned with colleagues, friends and family.”

Discounted hormone testing was offered, and 85 people registered within just 6 hours. Specialised GP consultations have also been popular, and attendees described them as “informative, helpful, and exactly what they needed”. These results indicate that colleagues trust the support available and feel empowered to address their needs.

Approximately 80 managers attended menopause training sessions over the past year, improving their understanding of symptoms, impacts and adjustment options. Managers report feeling better equipped to support colleagues and more aware of when to signpost to GP consultations.

L&Q has received ‘Menopause Friendly’ accreditation for its work in supporting colleagues affected by menopause. To achieve this quality mark, L&Q was assessed by an independent panel. It demonstrated effectiveness across its:

  • culture
  • policies and practices
  • training
  • engagement
  • working environment

The accreditation has given L&Q a clear, external benchmark to demonstrate the impact of its actions. It shows the programme is evidence-based and sends a clear message to current and future employees that L&Q takes menopause support seriously.

Efforts to support colleagues experiencing menopause helped L&Q win in the Female Health Advocacy Excellence category at the 2026 British Workplace Wellbeing Awards. The recognition also acknowledges L&Q’s work to create safe spaces for open conversations, improve women’s health education – including fertility support – and develop a wellbeing programme shaped by collaboration and colleague feedback.

Advice for other organisations

Manager capability has been a core focus throughout the journey. Practical guidance was developed to support managers in having empathetic, informed conversations, recognising that menopause symptoms can vary significantly and fluctuate over time. It has been reviewed on multiple occasions to reflect colleague feedback, learning from delivery and evolving organisational understanding.

For the majority, the most impactful backing will come from their manager. Many colleagues are afraid of talking about their struggles, for fear of being seen as inadequate or incapable. Psychological safety enables people to ask for adjustments, without judgement or penalty. This does not mean managers need to understand every detail of their team member’s circumstances. What makes the difference is patience and a willingness to be solutions focused. Simple actions, like checking in or covering meetings, can go a long way.

The accreditation process has helped formalise collaboration across departments, including HR, wellbeing, EDI, and health and safety. Roles and responsibilities are clearer, resources are more centralised, and menopause is an ongoing organisational priority rather than a standalone wellbeing topic.

Visible leadership support has also played a crucial role in establishing a new cultural norm. Featuring menopause in our ‘Exec cast’, an organisation-wide communication channel, was a turning point. When change comes from the top, it sends a powerful message that talking gives way to action. For champions and supporting teams to succeed in their efforts, and help shape a supportive environment, they should be complemented with a cultural and mindset shift from above.

Employees now have several options for menopause support. The ethos underpinning this approach is an acceptance that one size does not fit all. Each person experiencing menopause comes with their own unique set of experiences. By listening to every voice, an organisation can create meaningful, measurable and lasting change.

When evaluating the success of initiatives, stories are just as important as the numbers. Quantitative data can only go so far and needs to be supplemented with quantitative information – individual accounts and testimonies.

Although menopause adjustments are not formally tracked, informal accommodations are happening more frequently. Colleagues feel more able to ask for what they need, and managers are more confident offering flexibility, breaks, ventilation support and hybrid working arrangements.

Kalia Hylands, Chair of L&Q’s Menopause Champions said:

“One of the most striking things on this journey has been how much people want to talk once a safe space is created. We’ve seen colleagues who once felt isolated suddenly realise they’re not alone, and managers gaining confidence simply because they now know where to start.”
