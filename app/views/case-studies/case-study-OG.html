Opportunity Green: Supporting staff with menopause and menstrual health in the workplace

At a glance
Employer name Opportunity Green
Employer sector Charity
Number of employees Less than 250
Action category Menopause
Summary of action and implementation Opportunity Green, a women-led not-for-profit, created a supportive workplace environment for employees experiencing menopausal symptoms and menstrual ill health. Rather than introducing new leave entitlements, they reviewed existing policies, drafted clear guidance, and raised awareness through team training and educational activities. This approach destigmatised women’s health issues, opened up workplace conversations, and empowered staff and managers to utilise existing flexible working and sick leave options without guilt.

Background Information

Established in 2021, Opportunity Green is a fast-growing not-for-profit organisation that helps to identify and unlock opportunities to tackle climate change. We build ambitious coalitions, support climate vulnerable countries in international negotiations and find innovative legal pathways to fight climate change.

We are based in London and work with a hybrid and remote team, including colleagues in Europe. We are a growing team, currently made up of 30 staff, 85% of which are women. As a women-led organisation, it felt important to properly address support related to women’s health. This ensures every one of us can do their best for the cause we care about.

Before introducing the policy and our mini-education campaign, menopause was not talked about much in the workplace. The policies in place did not provide clarity regarding what support could be used when one was having a problematic period or experiencing brain fog. Some staff felt able to seek support and ask for time off, while others did not.

What they wanted to do

We wanted to ensure that everyone in the organisation felt supported to do their best work. We felt that there was not enough specific or targeted support around women’s health issues.

How they did it

We researched what other organisations are doing in this area, what the NHS advice is, and what is best practice. Alongside that, we reviewed our current policies on flexible working and sickness absence, and our ways of working as a team. This was to see where specific support for those experiencing menopause or menstrual ill health could come.

We identified that there are already many supportive practices and processes in place that would benefit people managing symptoms of menopause or menstrual ill health in the workplace. We considered whether to just edit existing policies but felt that more information and guidance was needed, particularly to enable managers to support colleagues. We wrote a new policy which gave a summary of existing policies and support that can be used to help. The policy also raises awareness about the impacts of menopause and menstrual health impacts in the workplace and sign posts to services and support.

The next step was raising awareness and introducing the policy to the whole team. We ran a one-hour session which included background information, using videos of people’s lived experiences. We also created a quiz to inform colleagues about menopause and menstrual ill health and how this can affect women and people who menstruate in the workplace.

The outcome

There is now increased awareness among the team of women’s health in general and how that can impact on them and others in the workplace, moving towards destigmatising the topics. People feeling more supported, understood and heard in the workplace – positive feedback following the team meeting training and policy introduction.

We have given colleagues clear permission to treat menstrual ill health as any other ill health and act accordingly. Anecdotal evidence suggests staff feel less pressure (self-imposed) and guilt around taking time off when unwell.

Managers now have the necessary basic information and signposting to services and support to better support their colleagues.

Advice for other organisations

We set out to create a new policy to support our mostly female team anticipating creating a new leave entitlement. When we reviewed our existing policies and approaches around flexible working and sick leave, it became clear that we already had the tools. What was needed was empowering people to use them to overcome their menstrual health and menopausal challenges. We did not need to reinvent the wheel, we needed to start the conversation, create awareness, bring different policy elements in one place and educate everyone about the basics of menopause and menstrual health challenges that might be affecting colleagues at work.

We recommend reviewing existing policies and options. It might be that your organisation already does a lot that is helpful, and you do not need to create new allowances or new rules, but simply give employees and managers permission to seek appropriate support for their health in these situations. Charities often have plenty of good policies in place so it’s about being clear that they can be used for this too.

We hosted a session to introduce the policy as part of our weekly team learning meetings. We used videos and a quiz to educate colleagues about the topics and highlight the importance of the policy to all, regardless of whether they are personally impacted. It went well and people said it felt important for us to speak about this topic and be broad in our inclusion focus and activities.
