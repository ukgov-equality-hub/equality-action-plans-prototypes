University of Greenwich: How University of Greenwich embedded menopause awareness and support for staff

At a glance
Employer name Univeristy of Greenwich
Employer sector Education
Number of employees 1,000 - 4,999
Action category Menopause
Summary of action and implementation The University of Greenwich normalised menopause awareness and support through a grass-roots, employee-led initiative. Starting with virtual "menopause cafés" in 2020, the movement gained leadership backing and partnered with Henpicked. The university embedded menopause guidance into workplace policies, delivered engaging staff training, and trained 25 volunteer menopause champions across faculties. This inclusive approach improved staff wellbeing, engaged employees across genders, and earned the team multiple regional and institutional awards for equality, diversity, and inclusion.

Background Information

Founded as the second polytechnic in the UK, the University of Greenwich is a multi-campus higher education institution based in London and the South East of England with over 26,000 students and 2,350 staff (HESA 2024/25).

The university strategy works towards 4 strategic priorities:

  • student success
  • inclusivity and culture
  • impactful research and knowledge exchange
  • connected and sustainable campuses

It is supported by 6 sub- and enabling strategies which embed sector-leading equality, diversity and inclusion (EDI), mental health and wellbeing.

The ’inclusivity and culture’ strategic priority aims to support and develop University of Greenwich staff, ensuring that their values (inclusivity, impactful and collaborative), culture and behaviours outline the things that matter as a community. One of these is to celebrate the diversity of the staff and students. The inclusive culture aims to foster an environment of freedom of expression and a place for staff and students to feel safe, accepted and recognised for who they are without fear of exclusion.

What they wanted to do

The University of Greenwich wanted to ensure that all colleagues were informed, educated and empowered to be able to make decisions about their own approach to their menopause journey and to help support them in their workplace. They also wanted colleagues who were also managers to be informed and educated and empowered to know how to support their staff. This would be achieved by creating a culture where the conversation about menopause was normalised and people felt supported through guidance, training and senior leadership ‘buy-in’.

How they did it

In autumn 2020, a colleague identified the need for staff experiencing menopause symptoms to be supported at work. This was partly because of the impact of the menopause on her own health and wellbeing at work. She could not believe she was the only person at the university struggling in silence. The colleague asked her manager for permission to invite colleagues in her faculty to an online chat and courageously spoke about her experience of menopause at work.

Between October and December 2020, there were 9 menopause online chats with faculties and directorates across the university. The colleague went on to send an open invite to all colleagues to a virtual menopause café in February 2021. The attendance was high, and women were thankful to her for sharing her story and providing this confidential space for others to share their stories. The conversation at the university began and in March 2021, the university’s Vice Chancellor gave approval to partnering with Henpicked: Menopause in the Workplace to raise further awareness of the menopause at the university. At the same time, a small menopause team was formed from members of staff across the university.

The approach taken reflected the university’s strategy for 2021 to 2030 which promotes an inclusive culture and encourages every person to bring their true self to work, and to feel a sense of belonging. Typically in organisations, menopause awareness is initiated by senior leaders. But the university’s approach developed organically, led by a member of staff in a non-leadership role, something that had a positive impact on engagement from all staff across the university. The work to foster an open conversation about menopause truly represents how the workplace culture and the lived experience intersect.

The positive feedback received by colleagues and line managers inspired the menopause team to go further and confront the taboo across the wider university. By introducing workplace menopause guidance, this put the word ‘menopause’ in writing for the first time. The guidance paved the way for the menopause to be incorporated into other policies. The university’s sickness absence policy and procedure includes guidance for managers on the impact of menopause and how to manage sickness absence triggers. The university’s heating and cooling policy was updated in September 2023 with a clear process for obtaining fans or supplementary heating for staff experiencing menopause symptoms. The new reasonable adjustments policy includes menopause as an adjustment not covered under the Equality Act. Managing performance and probation policies also link to menopause guidance.

The guidance gave the university the foundation to tackle the taboo around menopause head-on. To put the guidance into practice, the university developed a robust implementation plan to raise awareness, and educate and train staff. Since December 2021, training sessions have been delivered to all colleagues, including managers. The training sessions are informative, engaging and upbeat. They are run by the menopause team, who not only give information and signpost our support package, but also speak with honesty and openness. This makes it okay to talk about the menopause at work, whoever you are. Following the training initiatives, there was an overwhelming demand from university staff for further activities.

To further embed awareness and education, it was identified that consistency was needed. Guidance and training were not enough on their own. The conversation needed to keep going with the message ‘it’s okay to talk about menopause’ in ’menopause cafes’.

To maintain the conversation, several events and menopause cafes are hosted throughout the year, maximising the internal resources and taking an innovative approach to attracting external guest speakers to the events. It is ensured these events are accessible for all staff and managers by working with the communications team to publicise widely.

The benefit to staff of the menopause cafés has been significant. They have provided staff with a safe space to communicate and listen to others going through a similar journey and compare notes as well as using it to express what they need to support them in work. The cafés have also provided a platform to recruit volunteer menopause champions. The outcome was the recruitment and training of 25 menopause champions who, since June 2023, have provided localised support to colleagues and managers who are directly or indirectly impacted by menopause. Menopause champions are located across the university in each faculty or directorate and act as a point of contact, providing a listening ear and signposting support.

Awareness training was provided for volunteers to become menopause champions, and the university’s mental health first aiders also received menopause training. The university engages with an external occupational health provider, and a member of their team took up the offer of a place on the Henpicked Occupational Health Nurse Study Day.

It was recognised that everyone engages with information differently, so multiple communication channels to raise awareness and educate colleagues about menopause were provided. Initially, a menopause website was created with information, resources and links to internal and external events. A Menopause Teams Hub was created, providing an informal way for colleagues to share tips and support each other. It was also understood that some colleagues may prefer to seek support confidentially therefore a menopause email account is provided.

The menopause team realised they could go further with their campaign and share their knowledge with others outside of the university to help normalise the conversation about menopause. They realised that all people within all organisations need to be supported and this could not be achieved in isolation. They identified that if organisations work together to find the best solutions to progress menopause awareness and support, this will ensure lives improve for everyone experiencing menopausal symptoms whether directly or indirectly, within or outside of organisations. The ethos of the menopause team is that if they can help just one person or one organisation, even in a small way, they will be making a difference to the collective change that needs to happen and the ‘ripple effect’ will occur.

The outcome

The outcome was the start of a conversation about menopause, reassuring many women across the university that they were not alone. An unexpected benefit of starting this work was the realisation that our male colleagues wanted to engage with the conversation who realised this was happening to their colleagues, relations and partners.

The menopause team realised that what the university offers their staff needs to be consistent and sustainable long term. Along with the guidance is a robust package of training, resources, support, and events which has helped change the culture to normalise the menopause conversation. For example, it is common for menopause team members to have unplanned conversations about the menopause, both personal and professional, ranging from questions about symptoms to a request for training for team members.

Some indicators of the effectiveness of our guidance are:

  • how normal it has become to have a conversation about the menopause
  • overall interest and engagement
  • attendance at training and events
  • the desire for more

This has culminated in university colleagues nominating and shortlisting the menopause team for ‘Making a Difference’ in 2024, ‘Outstanding Team’ in 2023 and a menopause initiative winning the ‘Greenwich Excellence’ in the 2025 UoG Staff Awards.

The menopause team were also shortlisted for the 2024 London Higher award for ‘outstanding contribution to equality, diversity and inclusion in London’.

Advice for other organisations

When embedding menopause support, the University of Greenwich adopted a grass-roots approach where one person’s voice started the momentum. The conversations that followed were aligned with the university’s inclusivity strategy which helped achieve the buy-in from senior leaders. The menopause work was achieved within the university’s context, where the grass-roots approach was effective. Therefore, when planning the approach to embedding menopause support, the approach taken should be planned within the context of the specific organisation.

Keeping the momentum of menopause awareness can be a challenge. Some tips include:

  • making sure there are different activities planned throughout the year
  • thinking about how to reach different audiences
  • considering how menopause support and awareness can be integrated into other areas of support so there is a joined-up approach
