Thames Valley Police: Reasonable adjustments to support a colleague through menopause

At a glance
Employer name Thames Valley Police
Employer sector Public Sector
Number of employees >20,000
Action category Menopause
Summary of action and implementation This case study examines how Thames Valley Police (TVP) supported an experienced Crime Scene Investigator (CSI) who experienced severe menopause symptoms. Through tailored reasonable adjustments – such as lighter uniform fabrics, flexible working hours, a wheeled equipment case, and practical workstation changes – the CSI was able to manage her symptoms, remain in her role, and continue performing effectively. The case highlights the importance of inclusive, proactive workplace support in retaining skilled employees and preserving specialist expertise.

Background Information

The CSI is an experienced investigator with over a decade of service. Thames Valley Police (TVP) CSIs work in a demanding operational environment. The role requires physical stamina, high cognitive clarity, and the ability to work irregular hours. The profession also involves:

  • lifting equipment
  • wearing specialist clothing
  • documenting scenes accurately
  • maintaining concentration in unfamiliar or high‑pressure environments

The CSI was dealing with severe menopause symptoms that made routine tasks uncomfortable and exhausting. She faced:

  • overheating
  • excessive sweating
  • bloating
  • sleep disruption
  • brain fog
  • low mood
  • palpitations
  • dehydration
  • joint and muscle pain

Over time, the impact on her wellbeing became so severe that she considered leaving the job she loved. This risk is mirrored in national data on menopausal women leaving the workforce.

The CSI self‑referred into TVP's reasonable adjustments management process (RAMP). This uses the organisational passport to support staff who need workplace adjustments.

TVP has a particularly strong reason to prioritise this issue. Around 48% of its workforce is female, and almost half of new recruits in recent years have been women. A large proportion of women work across operational and specialist roles. The force has recognised this and has invested heavily in becoming a leader in this area.

What they wanted to achieve

The CSI wished to continue performing effectively in her role while managing debilitating menopause symptoms. Her goal aligned closely with national priorities. The government has made retaining older women in employment a primary aim. It notes that supporting menopausal women not only is vital for wellbeing but also for addressing skills shortages and boosting the economy. TVP fully supports this and is committed to being an employer of choice.

For the CSI, the aim was to remain safe, comfortable, and confident in her day‑to‑day duties. She needed workplace adjustments that would:

  • reduce physical discomfort
  • accommodate her disrupted sleep
  • support her memory and concentration
  • allow her to manage the physical and cognitive load associated with her job

How they did it

TVP ’s commitment to being an employer of choice is evidenced by the fact that it was the first police force in the UK to be independently accredited as Menopause Friendly. In 2023, it was further recognised by winning the national Menopause Friendly Employer of the Year Award, demonstrating outstanding impact in building a supportive culture, embedding menopause‑friendly policies, and providing specialist training.

Against this organisational backdrop, the CSI accessed support through the RAMP and the TVP passport. RAMP advisors hold confidential and detailed conversations with staff to create tailored solutions. This approach has been praised nationally for focusing on individual needs and avoiding one‑size‑fits‑all responses.

Practical adjustments included:

  • uniform changes, with lighter, stretch fabric trousers replacing thick, rigid trousers that exacerbated her overheating and bloating.
  • improved workstation comfort, achieved by relocating her locker and providing a USB fan despite the force’s hot‑desking policy.
  • flexible working, allowing later start times to compensate for severe sleep disturbance — a common menopause symptom known to reduce cognitive performance.
  • a wheeled, wipe‑clean case to avoid lifting and carrying equipment, easing joint and muscle pain.
  • a Dictaphone meeting evidential standards to support her note‑taking when memory issues or brain fog affected recall.
  • laminated checklists to ease cognitive load and support consistent preparation for scenes.
  • in‑person SOP update sessions, recognising that written updates could be harder to process when concentration was impaired.
  • modified assessment procedures, with the Quality Team reducing the volume of real‑time questioning during audits to support her focus.

These adjustments reflect broader best practice identified in national reviews, which highlight that simple, cost‑effective changes — such as uniform modifications, flexible hours, and improved communication — can have a significant impact on women experiencing menopause in the workplace.

The CSI benefited from full support from her line and second‑line managers, aligning with TVP’s strategic goal of fostering a workplace “where everyone can flourish.”

The outcome

The adjustments enabled the CSI to stay in a role she loved, echoing national goals to prevent experienced women from leaving the workforce. Her physical discomfort reduced significantly, and the tailored support helped restore her confidence, concentration, and wellbeing.

TVP’s menopause‑friendly culture ensured that the CSI no longer felt isolated or at risk of disciplinary action related to menopause symptoms. 8 in 10 menopausal women say their employer has not provided adequate training or policies, making TVP stand out as an exception rather than the rule.

By retaining the CSI, TVP preserved valuable specialist expertise, operational continuity, and years of organisational knowledge — exactly the outcome that national policy papers emphasise as essential for workforce stability and economic resilience.

Advice for other organisations

This case illustrates that menopause support is essential for retaining skilled employees. Research by the Fawcett Society highlights that:

  • 14% of menopausal women reduce their hours
  • 14% go part‑time
  • 8% avoid applying for promotion due to symptoms

This demonstrates the wide‑ranging impact on career progression when support is absent.

Organisations should:

  • create safe, confidential spaces for early conversations.
  • develop flexible, personalised adjustment pathways rather than rigid processes.
  • train managers thoroughly — a recognised strength of TVP’s approach.
  • review uniforms, work patterns, and equipment to ensure they do not exacerbate symptoms.
  • regularly update policies as the workforce evolves, drawing from TVP’s model, which has been nationally commended for innovation, inclusivity, and strong leadership commitment.

By learning from TVP’s example, organisations can:

  • improve retention
  • enhance staff wellbeing
  • create workplaces where menopausal women can continue to thrive
