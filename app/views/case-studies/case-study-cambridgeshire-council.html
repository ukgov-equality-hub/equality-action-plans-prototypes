Cambridgeshire County Council: How Cambridgeshire became a ‘Menopause Friendly’ employer

At a glance
Employer name Cambridgeshire County Council
Employer sector Public Sector
Number of employees 500 - 999
Action category Menopause
Summary of action and implementation Cambridgeshire County Council (CCC), employing 4,500 staff (75% women), enhanced its workplace support for colleagues experiencing menopause to achieve Menopause Friendly accreditation. Initiatives included year-round awareness campaigns, manager training, inclusive peer-support groups, and practical changes like providing emergency hygiene products. CCC also integrated menopause symptoms into absence management policies and introduced a Wellbeing Passport for reasonable adjustments. These measures improved employee wellbeing, fostered an inclusive culture, and enhanced recruitment and retention by establishing a supportive environment where staff feel empowered to thrive.

Background Information

Cambridgeshire County Council (CCC) employs around 4,500 people in diverse roles from highways engineers to social workers. 75% of our employees are women.

Our staff had significant variations in understanding, awareness, and experience relating to menopause. Some colleagues felt well supported by our existing employment policies and culture. But there was some progress to be made in ensuring a baseline awareness, and in hearing from colleagues about their experiences and any barriers that we could remove to them feeling that they could thrive at work, through their career and through different stages of life.

What they wanted to do

We had started on our journey to improving awareness of menopause. We decided that, as a key employer in our community, we should aim for external accreditation of our approach. This would ensure that we were aligned with best practice and accountable for high standards in our support to colleagues and respect for their lived experience.

How they did it

Through a range of actions, we worked steadily towards our goal to become accredited as a Menopause Friendly Employer. This included using a range of internal communication channels to ensure that information about menopause reached all employees. These channels included webinars, newsletters, and manager-specific channels. We sustained our awareness campaigns year-round, creating bespoke landing pages so that colleagues had a one-stop shop for information and resources. We shared case studies and personal stories to create more relatable, inclusive content.

One of our priorities is ensuring that menopause is seen as an issue that affects everyone, not just those experiencing it directly. Through our channels, we actively promote stories from all communities within our workforce to reflect the diverse nature of our employees. This includes sharing a personal experience from a male colleague who participated in a workshop, and encouraging senior leaders to openly discuss menopause in leadership forums and sharing their stories on our intranet.

Our external communications also reflect our commitment to being menopause friendly, making it clear to prospective employees that we prioritise inclusivity and support. Feedback from new employees highlights how our menopause-friendly policies have influenced recruitment. One employee said, "I disclosed my menopause at the interview stage and found CCC to be supportive and flexible in ways my previous employer was not." This feedback reflects how our menopause approach is making us an attractive workplace, helping with retention by cultivating an inclusive and supportive environment.

Understanding the needs and experiences of our employees is key to our engagement activity. We work closely with trade unions and our internal equality, diversity and inclusion (EDI) network to run peer-support groups. These groups allow experiences to be shared, and provide a clear route to raise and resolve issues or barriers that require corporate support. We seek feedback from attendees of our ‘Menopause Awareness workshops’, which are open to all employees. These workshops not only educate but also provide a safe space where colleagues can openly discuss their experiences. Trainers are encouraged to relay common themes or individual challenges raised during sessions, allowing us to identify and remove barriers. For example, feedback gathered during these sessions directly led to the provision of emergency period products in our work bases. Positive feedback, such as "It makes me feel my employer cares" and "It’s a relief to know they are available," illustrates how practical changes can make employees feel supported. The success of this initiative prompted us to include provision of incontinence products, addressing broader health needs.

We continually evaluate the impact of our menopause support initiatives by listening to feedback and tracking participation in support programmes. In response to feedback, we updated our absence management process to include menopause symptoms as a specific reason for absence. We also added this as a reason for absence on our payroll system to enable better reporting of and analysis of absence data. This allows us to better understand how menopause affects our employees, and tailor our support accordingly.

We have made specific provision within our guidance on reasonable adjustments to highlight examples of how simple changes can be made to support people more effectively through menopause. to the working environment, working patterns, or uniforms,. We have linked this with our Wellbeing Passport so that if colleagues move roles, the adjustments they need move with them. We have supported this with clear training to line managers on making reasonable adjustments, as well as menopause awareness sessions for managers.

We have received feedback from colleagues that indicate the impact of our menopause awareness sessions. One manager said: "I will ensure that I use the resources shared during training and deliver this down to my staff, ensuring that I make reasonable adjustments for each and every member of the team." Another said: "I felt very supported to share my own experiences, and overall it was one of the best training sessions I have attended." The training has also led to practical changes in the workplace. One manager said they now plan to “speak more openly with my team about their needs”, indicating an impact on workplace communication and support. This openness is key to nurturing a culture where menopause is no longer a taboo topic but a normal part of wellbeing discussions.

The outcome

By embedding menopause awareness and support into our workplace culture, we have ensured that all employees feel valued, supported, and empowered to thrive at every stage of life.

One of the most significant outcomes of our Menopause Friendly journey has been the positive impact on employee wellbeing. By openly addressing menopause and the challenges it can present, we have empowered employees to feel more comfortable seeking support. We have developed flexible working options, provided menopause-specific resources, and implemented policies that acknowledge the unique needs of individuals going through menopause. These initiatives have allowed colleagues to manage their symptoms while maintaining their engagement.

Employee feedback has indicated a marked improvement in how supported employees feel during menopause. It has highlighted the increased confidence employees now feel in discussing their experiences with managers, leading to a more inclusive and understanding workplace culture.

The journey towards becoming a Menopause Friendly organisation has also significantly enhanced inclusivity across CCC. Menopause can often be a source of stigma or misunderstanding, especially in the workplace. By opening conversations about menopause, we have normalised the experience and broken down the barriers that previously prevented individuals from discussing their health needs.

As a result, our workplace is more inclusive for employees of all genders and ages. For those experiencing menopause, we have created a space where they can access the support they need without fear of discrimination or judgment. This is particularly important in ensuring gender equality in career progression, as we are committed to preventing menopause from becoming a barrier to progression or performance.

By creating an environment where employees feel cared for and supported, they are better equipped to provide excellent service to the community, reflecting our commitment to public wellbeing.

Advice for other organisations

We are happy to talk with other councils about our approach to becoming a Menopause Friendly Employer. An important part of our approach is continuously listening to colleagues to understand their experiences, and making menopause part of the ongoing conversation within a culture that prioritises supporting people to be well and to belong in our workplace.
