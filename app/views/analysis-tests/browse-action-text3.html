Fanco Ltd. said:

FANCO LTD has strengthening its recruitment processes to reduce the potential for unconscious bias during candidate screening. Applications are been assessed consistently against clearly defined skills, experience and essential criteria relevant to each role. We are promoting structured shortlisting and provide appropriate guidance to managers involved in recruitment. Recruitment outcomes is monitored by gender, including applications, shortlisting and appointments, to identify potential differences and support continuous improvement. Our aim is to ensure recruitment decisions remain fair, transparent and based on individual merit and suitability for the role.

Intellectual Property Office said:

We have embedded measures to reduce the impact of unconscious bias in recruitment, including the use of blind sifting processes. Applicants are asked to remove identifiable information such as name, address, place of birth and education details from applications and CVs, helping to ensure that assessment is based on skills, experience and potential. Recruiting managers are also supported with structured guidance to promote fair and objective decision-making. These approaches help reduce the risk of bias influencing outcomes and support our commitment to equitable recruitment and progression.

Isle of Wight College said:

The College is committed to fair, objective and inclusive recruitment practices that provide equitable opportunities for all applicants. To reduce the potential for unconscious bias, all applications are anonymised during the shortlisting process, removing personal information that is not relevant to assessing suitability for the role.

Recruitment and selection decisions are based on objective, role-related criteria and supported by training for recruiting managers on inclusive recruitment, fair decision-making and unconscious bias.

The College will continue to review its recruitment practices to promote consistency, transparency and fairness, ensuring that talented candidates from all backgrounds have the opportunity to succeed.

Rayleigh Schools Trust said:

To foster inclusive recruitment across all leadership and operational tiers, the Trust has implemented the Every HR platform across all schools to enforce anonymised candidate screening for shortlisting panels.

Applications and supporting statements are automatically stripped of candidate names, contact details, gender, and personal data before being made available to interviewing managers. Shortlisting panels evaluate candidate submissions strictly on an anonymised, objective basis.

Eliminating potential unconscious bias during early-stage screening encourages a diverse candidate pool, particularly for senior teaching and leadership roles, supporting balanced progression into higher pay quartiles over time.

This issue was identified through review of our screening processes and awareness of the potential for unconscious bias at early recruitment stages. Currently, personal information such as names and nationality may be visible during CV screening. We plan to review our HR Information System (HRIS) recruitment functionality to determine whether anonymised applications can be enabled. Where system changes are not immediately possible, interim guidance will be reinforced to hiring managers to focus assessment strictly on skills, experience, and role‑related criteria.
