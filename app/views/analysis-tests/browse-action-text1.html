Acorn Mobility Services Ltd. said:

Acorn uses inclusive language in job descriptions and lists only essential requirements, promoting equal opportunities.

This removes unnecessary barriers that may discourage underrepresented groups from applying. By focusing on the skills genuinely needed for the role, we aim to attract a wider, more diverse talent pool.

Inclusive wording also signals that we value fairness and belonging, strengthening our employer brand and helping us hire the best person for the job.

Edinburgh Airport Limited said:

We continually evolve our job descriptions to reflect changing priorities. All job descriptions follow a standard template, which includes a DEI statement. They will always include the salary or range, and only ask for qualifications which are absolutely necessary. This is to make roles accessible to as wide a pool as possible. We also analyse the language to ensure it is gender neutral or feminine coded, as research shows masculine coded language deters females from applying.

We will monitor recruitment outcomes, including applications, shortlisting and appointments by gender, to identify potential barriers and support continuous improvement in our recruitment practices.

Woodgate and Clark Limited said:

We identified inclusive language as a potential barrier to attracting diverse candidates through an internal review of our recruitment approach and external good‑practice guidance. Our Applicant Tracking System (ATS) includes built‑in bias and gender‑coded language detection, which is used when drafting and publishing job descriptions and adverts. This ensures potentially exclusionary language is flagged and amended before roles are advertised. This approach is now embedded as standard practice to help widen appeal and reduce unintended bias at the attraction stage.

Embedded

Wyre Forest District Council said:

After reviewing our latest gender pay data, we have identified that roles within our upper middle quartile are predominantly filled by women. When analysing our recruitment data for the last 2 years, those same roles have applications that are almost 70% female compared to just over 30% male.

We therefore intend to utilise AI and ATS functionality to ensure our job adverts and role profiles do not contain language or phrases that would be considered to have gender bias in the hope that will create a more equal ratio of applicants.
