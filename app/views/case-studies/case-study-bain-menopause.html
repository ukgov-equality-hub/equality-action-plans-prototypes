Bain & Company UK: How Bain improved its support for employees experiencing menopause

At a glance
Employer name Bain & Company UK
Employer sector Consultancy
Number of employees >20,000
Action category Menopause
Summary of action and implementation Bain & Company UK implemented a comprehensive menopause support initiative to improve retention and foster an inclusive workplace culture. Elements include a dedicated standalone menopause policy, specialized training for HR professionals and line managers, flexible workplace adjustments (such as temperature control and schedule modifications), and clinical-grade support via the Peppy app. Additionally, the firm established peer-sharing sessions (‘Menopause and More’) and marked World Menopause Day. These measures created a consistent framework, reduced stigma, and ensured employees can access practical help with confidence.

Background Information

Bain & Company is one of the world’s leading global management consulting firms. It partners with businesses and organisations across the private, public, and not-for-profit sectors. This case study focuses on Bain & Company's UK business, which operates from its London office and employs approximately 1,200 people.

52% of staff in the London office are women. But, as is common in professional services, the senior consulting team has a higher percentage of men. Bain has set a target of achieving 45% women in client-facing senior management roles and, as of September 2025, has reached 40% representation – up from 28% in 2020. Reducing the gender pay gap and improving female representation at senior levels are central to the firm's broader diversity, equity and inclusion (DE&I) strategy.

The professional services sector has historically underinvested in menopause support, often treating it as a personal matter rather than a workplace issue. This has contributed to stigma, inconsistent treatment by line managers, and sometimes the loss of experienced female employees at a stage in their careers when they are at their most valuable. For women in their 40s and 50s, menopause can coincide with a critical period of career progression, making the risk to gender equality particularly acute. In a firm where the 10-year journey to a partner role is the central career pathway, retaining talented women through all life stages is not just a matter of fairness – it is a strategic priority.

Before formalising its approach, Bain & Company UK lacked a consistent framework for how managers should respond to employees experiencing symptoms. In addition, the routes to support available to employees were not clearly communicated. The organisation recognised that a more structured, policy-led approach was needed to ensure fairness, consistency, and genuine practical help.

What they wanted to achieve

Our main goal was to create a working environment where employees experiencing menopause:

  • felt safe to speak up
  • knew what support was available to them
  • could access practical adjustments without having to navigate the process alone

We wanted to reduce the stigma attached to menopause in the workplace and ensure that managers had the knowledge and tools to respond appropriately and consistently.

To shift culture as well as practice, the initiative targeted 3 groups:

  • employees experiencing menopause symptoms
  • their line managers
  • the broader workforce

Success would be measured by the establishment of clear policy, increased awareness among managers, and positive feedback from employees on the support available.

How they did it

The core of our approach was the introduction of a dedicated standalone menopause policy. Rather than folding menopause into general wellbeing or absence guidance, we gave it dedicated status – a deliberate signal that we take the issue seriously. The policy:

  • sets out the support available
  • clarifies the roles and responsibilities of managers and HR
  • provides practical routes to adjustments and specialist help

To support the policy, we invested in ensuring that HR professionals who handle confidential conversations were trained on menopause, its range of effects, and the support options available. This meant that employees had a knowledgeable, sensitively-trained point of contact they could approach without fear of being misunderstood or dismissed.

Managers received dedicated guidance to help them respond appropriately and sensitively when a team member raised menopause as an issue. This guidance aims to reduce the risk of an individual's experience depending on who their line manager happened to be. It covered:

  • how to have supportive conversations
  • how to agree on practical adjustments
  • how to follow up consistently

In terms of practical adjustments, we developed a flexible, individual-centred approach. Depending on a person’s needs, this could include:

  • temperature and ventilation support
  • changes to seating location
  • temporary adjustments to start and finish times
  • hybrid working
  • modifications to workload and deadlines during symptom flare-ups
  • access to quiet spaces for rest and recovery

Employees could also access occupational health for specialist advice, as well as an employee assistance programme (EAP) offering counselling and emotional support.

We also invested in clinical-grade specialist support through the Peppy app. This gives employees confidential, one-to-one access to:

  • menopause-trained clinicians
  • virtual consultations
  • structured support programmes
  • a library of content including articles, podcasts and expert-led virtual events

This sits alongside an in-house wellness team, including GPs and counsellors, providing another layer of accessible support. Culturally, Bain marked World Menopause Day each October with an organisation-wide communication. We also established a regular internal session called ‘Menopause and More’ as a space for experience-sharing and mutual support.

The outcome

The introduction of a standalone menopause policy, combined with manager guidance, practical adjustments, and clinical support, has given Bain UK a coherent and consistent framework for supporting employees through this stage of life. Employees now have clear, accessible routes to help, whether through HR, occupational health, the EAP, the in-house wellness team, or the Peppy app. Managers are better equipped to respond with confidence and sensitivity.

The ‘Menopause and More’ peer sessions have created a community for employees to share experiences and normalise conversations that might previously have gone unspoken. Celebrating World Menopause Day has reinforced the message that menopause is a legitimate workplace issue, not a private matter to be managed in silence.

Advice for other organisations

Professional services firms looking to implement similar changes should start by giving menopause its own dedicated policy rather than treating it as a subset of general health guidance. Standalone status sends a clear signal and ensures accountability. Pair the policy with concrete, practical guidance for managers – training alone is not sufficient if managers do not have a clear process to follow when a conversation arises.

Think carefully about the range of adjustments that are genuinely feasible in your environment, and make these explicit rather than leaving it to individuals to ask. Many employees will not know what to request, and the burden of initiating that conversation is already high. Making the options visible and normal reduces that barrier considerably.

Finally, do not underestimate the value of peer-to-peer connection. Formal policy and clinical support are essential, but many employees find it equally important to hear from colleagues who have navigated similar experiences. Creating structured but informal spaces for that – such as internal experience-sharing sessions – can be a low-cost, high-impact complement to your formal offer.
