Thames Valley Police: Reasonable adjustments to support a colleague through menopause

At a glance
Employer name Thames Valley Police
Employer sector Public Sector
Number of employees >10,000
Action category Menopause
Specific action Offer workplace adjustments for employees experiencing menopause
Summary of action and implementation To support a Crime Scene Investigator (CSI) managing menopause symptoms, the organisation implemented tailored workplace adjustments. These included uniform changes, workstation modifications (such as a fan and locker relocation), flexible working hours to accommodate sleep disturbance, ergonomic equipment (a wheeled case), and procedural support (such as Dictaphone use, checklists, and in-person SOP sessions) to assist with cognitive load.

Background Information

The CSI is an experienced Crime Scene Investigator with over a decade of service. TVP CSIs work in a demanding operational environment that requires physical stamina, high cognitive clarity, and the ability to work irregular hours. The profession also involves lifting equipment, wearing specialist clothing, documenting scenes accurately, and maintaining concentration in unfamiliar or high‑pressure environments.

The CSI, who has over a decade of operational experience, was dealing with severe menopause symptoms that made routine tasks uncomfortable and exhausting. She faced overheating, excessive sweating, bloating, sleep disruption, brain fog, low mood, palpitations, dehydration, and joint and muscle pain. Without intervention, she was close to leaving the job she loved — a risk mirrored in national data on menopausal women leaving the workforce.

Over time, the impact on her wellbeing became so severe that she considered leaving the job she loved. She therefore self‑referred into Thames Valley Police’s Reasonable Adjustments Management Process (RAMP), which uses the organisational passport to support staff who require workplace adjustments.

Thames Valley Police (TVP) has a particularly strong reason to prioritise this issue: around 48% of its workforce is female, and almost half of new recruits in recent years have been women. With a substantial proportion of women working across operational and specialist roles, the force has recognised the need to improve workplace support for those experiencing menopause and has invested heavily in becoming a leader in this area.

What did we want to achieve?

The CSI wished to continue performing effectively in her role while managing debilitating menopause symptoms. Her goal aligned closely with national priorities: the UK Government has made retaining older women in employment a key objective, noting that supporting menopausal women is vital not only for wellbeing but also for addressing skills shortages and boosting the economy. TVP fully supports this and is committed to being an employer of choice.

For the CSI, the aim was to remain safe, comfortable, and confident in her day‑to‑day duties. She needed workplace adjustments that would reduce physical discomfort, accommodate her disrupted sleep, support her memory and concentration, and allow her to manage the physical and cognitive load associated with crime scene attendance and documentation.

How we did it

TVP’s commitment to being an employer of choice is evidenced by the fact that it was the first police force in the UK to be independently accredited as Menopause Friendly. In 2023, it was further recognised by winning the national Menopause Friendly Employer of the Year Award, demonstrating outstanding impact in building a supportive culture, embedding menopause‑friendly policies, and providing specialist training.

Against this organisational backdrop, the CSI accessed support through the Reasonable Adjustments Management Process (RAMP) and the TVP passport. RAMP advisors hold confidential and detailed conversations with staff to create tailored solutions — an approach praised nationally for avoiding one‑size‑fits‑all responses and instead focusing on individual need.

Practical adjustments included:

  • Uniform changes, with lighter, stretch fabric trousers replacing thick, rigid trousers that exacerbated her overheating and bloating.
  • Improved workstation comfort, achieved by relocating her locker and providing a USB fan despite the force’s hot‑desking policy.
  • Flexible working, allowing later start times to compensate for severe sleep disturbance — a common menopause symptom known to reduce cognitive performance.
  • A wheeled, wipe‑clean case to avoid lifting and carrying equipment, easing joint and muscle pain.
  • A Dictaphone meeting evidential standards to support her note‑taking when memory issues or brain fog affected recall.
  • Laminated checklists to ease cognitive load and support consistent preparation for scenes.
  • In‑person SOP update sessions, recognising that written updates could be harder to process when concentration was impaired.
  • Modified assessment procedures, with the Quality Team reducing the volume of real‑time questioning during audits to support her focus.

These adjustments reflect broader best practice identified in national reviews, which highlight that simple, cost‑effective changes — such as uniform modifications, flexible hours, and improved communication — can have a significant impact on women experiencing menopause in the workplace.

The CSI benefited from full support from her line and second‑line managers, aligning with TVP’s strategic goal of fostering a workplace “where everyone can flourish.”

The outcome

The adjustments enabled the CSI to stay in a role she loved, echoing national goals to prevent experienced women from leaving the workforce. Her physical discomfort reduced significantly, and the tailored support helped restore her confidence, concentration, and overall wellbeing.

TVP’s menopause‑friendly culture ensured that the CSI no longer felt isolated or at risk of disciplinary action related to menopause symptoms — a key barrier noted in national reports, which show that eight in ten menopausal women say their employer has not provided adequate training or policies, making TVP stand out as an exception rather than the rule.

By retaining the CSI, TVP preserved valuable specialist expertise, operational continuity, and years of organisational knowledge — exactly the outcome that national policy papers emphasise as essential for workforce stability and economic resilience.

Advice for other organisations

This case illustrates that menopause support is not optional: it is essential for retaining skilled employees. The Fawcett Society’s research highlights that 14% of menopausal women reduce their hours, 14% go part‑time, and 8% avoid applying for promotion due to symptoms, demonstrating the wide‑ranging impact on career progression when support is absent.

Organisations should:

  • Create safe, confidential spaces for early conversations.
  • Develop flexible, personalised adjustment pathways rather than rigid processes.
  • Train managers thoroughly — a recognised strength of TVP’s approach.
  • Review uniforms, work patterns, and equipment to ensure they do not exacerbate symptoms.
  • Regularly update policies as the workforce evolves, drawing from TVP’s model, which has been nationally commended for innovation, inclusivity, and strong leadership commitment.

By learning from TVP’s example, organisations can improve retention, enhance staff wellbeing, and create workplaces where menopausal women — a key and growing demographic — can continue to thrive.
